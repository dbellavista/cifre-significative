# Correlation discovery framework

Code: `src/cifresignificative/studies/italy_productivity/analysis/corr.py` (functions), `experiments/` (configs + runner), notebook `24_correlation_discovery`, dashboard view *Correlation discovery*, tests `tests/studies/italy_productivity/test_corr.py`. Results land in `data/processed/correlations/*.parquet` (gitignored; `experiments.run_all()` rebuilds them in ~15 s). Everything is a descriptive association, never causal.

## Funnel
1. **Feature table.** `panel_matrix` drops flagged observations (`b`, `p`, `backcast`, `linked`); `cross_section` makes country-window means or changes; `within_changes` makes year-on-year differences demeaned by country *and* year.
2. **Exclusions.** Size-scaled series (counts, EUR millions, `_ths`/`_nr`/`_meur`, population), same-quantity pairs (`wb_x`/`x`, `arope`/`arope_old`, same two-token family, `KNOWN_DUPLICATES`), and survivors with |rho| >= 0.95.
3. **Screen.** `pair_screen` (Spearman default, ranks on each pair's complete rows, n >= 20), then Benjamini-Hochberg `fdr` at q = 5%.
4. **Robustness.** `robustness`: every leave-one-country-out rho keeps its sign and >= 50% of |rho|; |rho| without Ireland >= 50%; Pearson/Spearman agree in sign.
5. **Null check.** `permutation_null` shuffles each indicator across countries; expect ~0 discoveries.

## Known limits
- Rich countries are better on almost everything; raw levels mostly rediscover that, hence the income-controlled default for E1.
- Name-based duplicate filters are heuristic; the top of each list still needs human reading. Robustness checks rarely reject strong FDR survivors.
- ~30 countries, 15-20 years: E5 has n = 14-20 yearly gap changes, so it has little power.
- E4 shows predictive order only. Regional (NUTS2) and sector tables are not yet included.

## Regional (NUTS2) screen
Code: `src/cifresignificative/studies/italy_productivity/analysis/regional_corr.py`, runners `experiments.run_regional_all()` (about 5 s), notebook `25_regional_correlation_discovery`, dashboard tab *Regions (NUTS2)*, tests `tests/studies/italy_productivity/test_regional_corr.py`. About 230 regions with data in 2015-19, 26 Eurostat NUTS2 indicators (`EUROSTAT_REGIONAL` in `catalog.py`) plus derived ratios (output per hour, hours per inhabitant, investment rate, labour share, pay per hour).

Differences from the national screen:
- **Unit and demeaning.** Each indicator minus its country mean (countries with fewer than 3 regions with data drop out), then the log-GDP-per-head fit removed.
- **Dependence.** Neighbouring regions are not independent, so robustness drops *whole countries* (`corr.robustness(groups=...)`), the permutation null shuffles regions *within* countries (`corr.shuffle_within`), and panel models cluster SEs by country (`corr.panel_fe(entity_level="geo", cluster_country=True)`).
- **Mechanical pairs.** Derived ratios that share GDP, hours or population are never paired with each other (`rc.MECHANICAL`); size-scaled totals (`_mio_eur`, `_ths`, population) are excluded.
- **Flags.** Flags are strings such as `bp` or `bdu`; observations are dropped if the flag contains any character in `drop_flag_chars` (`u` for single-window levels; `ubp` for changes and panels, since breaks matter when comparing across time). `ZZ` extra-regio codes are removed at build time.

| id | what | method |
|---|---|---|
| R1 `r1_levels_within` / `r1_levels_between` | 2015-19 within-country regions / country means of regions | Spearman + BH-FDR + leave-one-country-out |
| R2 `r2_changes_within` | change 2005-09 to 2015-19, within countries | same |
| R3 `r3_region_panel`, `r3_lead_lag` | regional analogues of the literature hypotheses (`rc.HYPOTHESES`, FDR over these only) plus the top robust R1 pairs from different themes (`S` rows, not independent evidence); region + year FE, both lead-lag directions at 1-3 years | `panel_fe` |
| R4 `r4_italy_slope` | Italy's within-country slope vs all other countries, ranked against the same difference for every country with at least 5 regions (p = share with an equal or larger |difference|; the smallest possible p is 1 / number of countries); Italy North vs South split | placebo ranking |
| R5 `r5_convergence` | beta-convergence of GDP per head (PPS), country effects, with and without the richest region per country | OLS, SEs clustered by country |

Known limits: about 27 countries act as clusters despite ~230 regions; NUTS codes mix vintages (coverage differs by indicator, e.g. AROPE 137 regions and starts 2015, broadband ends 2021, physicians end 2020); output per hour is at current prices, so only within-country comparisons are reliable; Italy is a single cluster, so R4 gives a ranking, not a significance test.

## Why the South: two designs side by side
Code: `src/cifresignificative/studies/italy_productivity/analysis/regional_south.py`, runner `experiments.run_south_all()` (about 2 minutes, writes `s*.parquet` to `data/processed/correlations/`), notebook `34_regional_south`, tests `tests/studies/italy_productivity/test_regional_south.py`. Data: EQI waves and Eurostat structural business statistics in `regional_extra.parquet` (kept apart from `regional.parquet` so the test counts of the notebook 25 screens do not change), Istat internal migration and CPT spending as Italy-only tables.
- **Track A, EU-wide (S1, S2).** Pre-specified family: EQI wave 2017 (composite and three components) and three SBS indicators against six regional gaps, outcomes averaged over 2015-19 (waves 2010, 2013, 2021 with their own windows are replications); regions demeaned by country; Spearman with **within-country permutation p-values** (`perm_p`), BH over the family, leave-one-country-out; variant with the log-income fit removed from every non-income variable. Pay per person employed against income or output per hour is near-definitional and excluded (`rs.MECHANICAL`). S2 ranks Italy's slope among the other countries' (`rc.italy_slope_test`).
- **Why permutation p-values.** The asymptotic Spearman p on country-demeaned, pooled data over-rejects a shuffled test (8-9% at a nominal 5%): countries differ in dispersion, which widens the null of the pooled rho. The shuffled family (100 shuffles) gives at least one discovery in 2 and p < 0.05 in 5.6% of tests. The notebook 25 screen keeps the asymptotic p in its funnel, and section R2b retests every pair with the permutation p (`rs.screen_perm`, `ex.run_regional_perm_check`): 164 of 165 levels and 53 of 59 changes survivors remain; on 50 shuffles of the levels table the asymptotic p is below 0.05 in 6.1% of pairs (0.12 discoveries per run) and the permutation p in 5.0% (0).
- **Track B, Italy only (S3, S5).** 21 regions, one cluster, so no placebo countries: Spearman across regions with a permutation p (values shuffled across all regions) and a within-zone version (North 9, Centre 4, South and Islands 8: values demeaned by zone and shuffled within zones), leave-one-region-out range, BH over the family. Exploratory: indicators were chosen by eye, the zone split removes real North-South differences as well as confounding, and power is low.
- **S4** puts the two answers side by side for the indicators that exist EU-wide (Italy's rho is the same number in both; the reference differs). **S5** gives the South-minus-North gap in standard deviations.
Known limits: EQI is perception-based, mixes NUTS1 and NUTS2 (only NUTS2 kept, Germany and Belgium missing) and has five waves; SBS ends in 2020 and counts persons employed including the self-employed; CPT attributes spending to where it is incurred (pensions are about 28% of the total); migration is registry transfers with no education split; no causal reading.

## Daily spread and rating changes
Code: `src/cifresignificative/studies/italy_productivity/analysis/daily.py`, runner `experiments.run_daily_all()` (about 5 s), notebook `27_daily_spreads_ratings`, tests `tests/studies/italy_productivity/test_daily.py`. Data: daily ITA and DEU 10-year yields (`data/external/source/daily_yields/`, manual export, kept in a separate table because the `highfreq` contract is monthly/quarterly) and 40 hand-collected rating changes (all `verified` against the agencies' own records; Spain, France, Portugal and Greece daily yields are loaded but not yet used) (`data/external/rating_actions.csv`, evidence-labelled).

Safeguards:
- **Windows in trading days.** Change in the ITA-DEU spread over [t-1, t+h] for h = 1, 5, 10, t = first trading day on or after the event (announcements may come after the close); weekend quotes are dropped.
- **Volatility normalisation.** The change is divided by the trailing 60-day standard deviation of daily spread changes times sqrt(h+1), so 2011-12 and 2020 do not dominate; a 20-day window is the robustness check.
- **Placebo.** Exact two-sided p over all days whose window overlaps no event (`daily._blocked`); events on the same date are one test (`experiments.daily_events`); BH-FDR over every (event date, horizon) test.
- **Pooled direction-aligned test** for rating changes (`daily.pooled_signed`): mean of direction x z against random non-event days with the same signs, one-sided; robustness over the volatility window, over evidence level (the distinct levels of the `unverified` < `partly verified` < `verified` ladder; every row is `verified` now, so there is one) and leaving out 2011-12 or 2020-21; BH-FDR over the pooled tests (6 now).
- **Null.** `daily.placebo_discoveries`: random dates, same procedure, 200 repetitions; must give about zero discoveries.

|---|---|
| D1 `d1_events` | 279 tests: 93 event dates (governments, policies, ECB decisions, rating changes) x 3 horizons on the daily ITA-DEU spread |
| D2 `d2_ratings_pooled` | pooled signed test of the 40 rating changes (12 rows: evidence x volatility window x horizon) |

## High-frequency (monthly / quarterly) dynamics
Code: `src/cifresignificative/studies/italy_productivity/analysis/hf.py`, runners `experiments.run_hf_all()` (about 5 s), notebook `26_high_frequency_dynamics`, dashboard tab *Monthly / quarterly*, tests `tests/studies/italy_productivity/test_hf.py`. Data: 12 monthly and 4 quarterly Eurostat series (`EUROSTAT_HIGHFREQ` in `catalog.py`, `freq` field on `Indicator`), fetched into `data/raw/eurostat_highfreq/` and built into `data/processed/highfreq.parquet` (`iso3, geo, period, date, freq, indicator, value, flag, theme, source`; `date` is the period end). The annual panel and its vintages are not touched.

Safeguards:
- **Stationarity.** Each series is transformed (`hf.TRANSFORMS`: change, log change, or level when already stationary) and checked with ADF + KPSS (`hf0_stationarity`; levels of the HICP rate and of labour-cost growth are shown for contrast). Nothing is correlated in levels.
- **Autocorrelation.** Cross-correlations are prewhitened (AR by BIC, same filter on both series); panel Granger tests add the dependent series' own lags, country and month effects, and country-clustered errors; the spread model uses Newey-West errors, by regime (2005-2011, 2012-2019, 2020+).
- **Gaps.** Panels keep the complete date grid so row-based lags never jump over a missing month.
- **Events.** `hf.event_study` compares the six months after an event with the six months before, against every other month at least six months away (exact placebo distribution, +1 smoothing); BH-FDR over events x series.
- **Italy.** One country, so Italy's rank among the others (`hf.italy_rank`) replaces a significance test.

| id | what |
|---|---|
| H0 `hf0_stationarity` | ADF/KPSS summary per series and transformation |
| H1 `hf1_spread` | monthly change in the spread over Germany (ITA, ESP, FRA, PRT, GRC) on lagged differentials with Germany, by regime |
| H2 `hf2_panel`, `hf2_countries`, `hf2_italy` | sentiment -> production/retail -> unemployment, GDP -> employment/hours: panel Granger verdicts, per-country best lead, Italy's rank |
| H3 `hf3_passthrough_*` | wage (labour-cost) pass-through of inflation, 2005Q1-2021Q2 vs the 2021Q3-2023Q4 energy shock, Italy's rank |
| H4 `hf4_events` | 57 dated events (Italian governments, policies, ECB decisions in `data/external/ecb_events.csv`) x 4 Italian series |

Known limits: HICP comes from the ECOICOP-2 dataset (`prc_hicp_minr`) because `prc_hicp_manr` ends in 2025-12; ECB event dates are labelled by evidence in the CSV (only the July 2022 decision is verified against an official-style source, the OMT date rests on a single source); events cluster at crisis turning points, so they cannot be separated from the cycle; GBR, USA and JPN are largely absent from the monthly yield series; Eurostat's seasonal adjustment is taken as given.

## NUTS 2021 geography and spatial dependence (notebook 37)
Code: `src/cifresignificative/core/clean/nuts.py` (crosswalk, `harmonise`, `overlap_agreement`, `coverage`), loader `sources/gisco_nuts.py` (GISCO 20M level-2 boundaries 2010, 2013, 2016, 2021, 2024 in `data/raw/gisco_nuts/`), `src/cifresignificative/studies/italy_productivity/analysis/spatial.py` (contiguity, Moran's I, eigenvector filtering, calibration null), runners `experiments.run_nuts_all()` (`n*.parquet`) and `run_spatial_all()` (`sp*.parquet`, about 1 minute), tests `tests/core/test_nuts.py`, `tests/studies/italy_productivity/test_spatial.py`. Outputs of the build: `regional_nuts2021.parquet`, `regional_extra_nuts2021.parquet` (with `geo_old`, `geo_method`).
- **Crosswalk.** Area overlap of each old region with the 2021 regions (relative to the old area covered by any 2021 region, because generalised boundaries leave coastal slivers): `same`, `nested` (at least 95% inside one 2021 region: renamed or merged), `split` (left out, no value is invented), `later` (code exists only from 2024, left out); NUTS 2006 codes (Istat ITD/ITE, FI1A) are mapped by hand in `LEGACY_2006` and FI18 is a split. The data contain no true merger (France 2016 and Greece 2013 are relabelling), so nothing is summed. The native 2021 row wins over a renamed one in transition years; the old and new values agree closely (`overlap_agreement`).
- **Spatial structure.** Queen contiguity within a country (2 km tolerance for the generalised borders; islands linked to the nearest same-country region; cross-border links ignored). Moran's I of country-demeaned columns; eigenvector spatial filtering (Griffith): candidates are eigenvectors of the country-centred weights with eigenvalue at least 25% of the largest, chosen greedily until the standardised Moran's I of the remainder is below 1.645 (at most 20).
- **Tests.** SP2 reruns the notebook 34 family plain and filtered (within-country permutation p, BH over the family each way); SP3 recomputes Italy's rank among countries on filtered columns; SP4 calibrates both pipelines with independent spatially smooth x (autocorrelation 0.4 and 0.8) and real y: plain 10.7% and 25.0% of tests with p < 0.05 (0.43 and 4.08 false discoveries per family), filtered 6.5% and 6.6% (0.06 and 0.11).
- **Reading.** The filter removes spatial gradients shared by both variables, including a North-South gradient within a country, so a pair that fails after filtering may be explained by that gradient or over-filtered; report both. The Italy-only track (21 regions) is not filtered.
- **Limits.** 20M boundaries; same-country neighbours only; ad hoc stopping rule and a first-order autoregressive calibration pattern; area overlap as a proxy for population overlap; Germany and Belgium absent from EQI; columns with negative autocorrelation (physicians, fertility after filtering) are not addressed. **Notebook 25 screens rerun** (`experiments.run_spatial_screens`, results `sp5_screen_pairs`, `sp6_screen_counts`, `sp7_screen_null`, about 6 minutes with 30 simulations; not part of the CI refresh): the levels and changes pair screens (`regional_south.screen_perm`, BH over all pairs) on the original codes, on NUTS 2021 and after spatial filtering of every column: 164/53 survivors, 169/57 and 159/46 (152/44 in common; lost pairs have median |rho| 0.18/0.25, no kept pair changes sign). Whole-screen null: shuffled within countries 0 discoveries both ways; independent smooth noise with Moran's I about 0.16: 0 both ways; about 0.57: plain 16.9 (levels) and 12.7 (changes) discoveries per simulation, filtered 0.

## Budget-law financing: coverage ratios and the link to deficit and debt (notebook 38)
Code: `src/cifresignificative/core/sources/budget_laws.py` (download, parser, accounting identities, `budget_laws.csv`), `src/cifresignificative/studies/italy_productivity/analysis/budget.py` (measures), tests `tests/studies/italy_productivity/test_budget_laws.py`; data `data/external/budget_laws.csv`, table choice `data/external/budget_laws_tables.csv` (with a title guard). Source: the dossiers "Effetti sui saldi e conto risorse e impieghi" of the Servizi Bilancio of Camera and Senato, one per budget law, 2016-2026 (2022: gross split rebuilt from the measure lines, see below).
- **Measures** (net borrowing aggregate, EUR million and % of GDP): uses (impieghi), resources (risorse), balance = resources - uses, **coverage ratio** = resources / uses, **deficit-financed share** = 1 - coverage, the split of the resources into higher revenue and lower spending, the share of the uses that is higher spending; per horizon year (h = 0 is the year the law acts on) and summed over the three years. Scopes: `bill`, `budget` (bill plus the decree-law of the same dossier: 2020 and 2024) and `pandemic` (kept apart). Each manovra is attributed to the government in office when the draft budgetary plan goes to Brussels (15 October of the previous year).
- **Checks**: 165+ accounting identities (uses = higher spending + lower revenue, resources = higher revenue + lower spending, balance = resources - uses) hold for every parsed table; the 2022 net summary adds up; 2025 was also checked against the page image.

### Municipal declared-income source follow-up

Source income per recipient and recipient ratios use overlapping fiscal frequencies: they are not mutually exclusive population shares and do not follow the same people. The identity amount/common-denominator = recipient-ratio × amount/recipient can support an accounting split only after component totals, missing cells, tax definitions and a common price basis are reconciled. With unreconciled components, report them separately and do not label the residual as an economic 'other income' explanation. A common national deflator preserves city growth ranks; chaining published rounded annual rates can differ from direct annual-index endpoints, and changing ECOICOP vintage must remain an explicit sensitivity or documented revision.
